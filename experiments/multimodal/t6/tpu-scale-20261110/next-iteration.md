# Training priorities after G6

## What the scaling run establishes

The 202.35M hybrid had plausible optimization headroom on simulated dynamics. From 2,048 to 8,192 updates its development endpoint errors fell from 12.04 to 6.52 pixels and from 12.32 to 6.47 across the two seeds. Its deficit to the 116.50M hybrid narrowed from 1.38 and 1.34 pixels to 0.17 and 0.16. Both final size-difference intervals include zero. The last interval spans a fourfold increase in exposure. It cannot determine the slope at the final update, a crossover point, or the compute-optimal size. Both sizes received one learning-rate schedule and the same 134.2M multimodal positions per model. [Matched checkpoints and intervals](matched.md) · [Training protocol](protocol.md)

Real contact timing gives a different answer. Over that same interval the larger hybrid’s training average precision rose from 0.161/0.151 to 0.810/0.802, while held-date scores fell from 0.0548/0.0577 to 0.0420/0.0468. Raw frame motion scored 0.0581 on held dates. The mixture exposed each available impact window about 45 times at the terminal budget, drawn from 22 training dates. These are substantial repetitions of a small number of environments. [Contact audit](contact-generalization.md) · [Exposure counts](../../t5/interventional-world-20261110/metrics/data-exposure.json) · [Measured results](report.md)

The hybrid’s strongest advantage is action-conditioned prediction. On 512 fresh scenes the 202M hybrid scored 6.12/6.01 pixels against dense at 8.14/7.41. Giving it the correct camera history also improved action choice against erased and wrong-scene video with the same candidate plans. The larger hybrid scored 2.01 bits per held text byte against 2.16–2.20 for dense and passed a narrow four-command cross-view transfer gate on eight held speakers. Performance varied by direction, and the second seed’s absolute accuracy was 25.81% against 25% chance. Both architectures have the same 128-dimensional bottleneck, edge modules and physical priors. This comparison supports the hybrid temporal core within that design. An equal-budget untied-state control would help isolate the contribution of sharing itself. All learned speech conditions scored 100% word error on the frozen recognizer, against 51.56% on original audio and 65.63% on the codec reconstruction. ## Proposed comparisons

1. **Resolve data scarcity versus too few updates.** Continue paired 116M and 202M hybrid and dense checkpoints for the same additional schedule, for example to 16,384 total updates. At each size compare replay of the original mixture with the same family proportions filled with additional independent examples. Keep the continuation learning rate and checkpoint cadence common. Report results at equal examples and equal measured TPU-hours. Reserve new recording dates, speakers and simulator configurations for one final comparison. An improvement specific to fresh data would justify expanding that source. A reproducible size advantage at the increased budget would justify the extra parameters.

2. **Give the shared state an identifiable content target.** On a fixed data mix, compare the existing acoustic reconstruction objective with masked prediction of training-derived speech units or aligned phonemes. Use synchronized views of the same event, same-speaker wrong utterances, and within-recording time shifts. Align the content and timing the views can both reveal. Let generation heads model speaker timbre, texture and uncertainty when several outputs fit the observation. The staged 75,139 audio–transcript pairs can supervise linguistic content. New synchronized audiovisual sessions and independent contact recordings are needed for the visual claim. Decode ground-truth acoustic features, then oracle and predicted content units with the same renderer to localize errors to representation, timing or synthesis. Track word error, every frozen transfer direction, and correct-versus-shifted performance on unseen people and sites. [New data inventory](libri360-card.json) · [AV-HuBERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02184)

3. **Train memory on decisions that require history.** Start with the 116M hybrid and matched dense model. Train on contiguous decision sequences with executed actions and multi-step future observations. Include paired episodes with identical current observations but an earlier cue that determines which action will succeed. In G6 the fixed planner was already given the goal and a recent observation window. On 24 decisions the 202M carried-state hybrid finished at 85.97/117.59 pixels. Resetting at each decision yielded 65.54/71.30. Challenge carried memory against reset, shuffled history, a passive policy and an oracle that retains the cue. Evaluate complete episodes with new goals and transitions, and retain the fully observed task as a control. [Live controls](transfer-and-decisions.md) · [Action-conditioned latent post-training in V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985)

4. **Post-train the decision head in stages.** G6 uses a fixed planner to rank ten proposed plans. First freeze the world model and fit a small goal-conditioned scorer to realized outcomes of executed candidate plans. Use demonstrations and labeled recovery states for supervised fine-tuning when they are available. Compare with an equally sized raw-state head and a head on the dense world model. Then unfreeze small adapters or the last shared blocks in a separate arm, retaining a stream of multimodal prediction examples and checking for forgetting. Once supervised action ranking works on fresh scenes, compare it with a policy optimized for actual simulator success on states the policy visits. With fixed logged trajectories, offline RL such as IQL is a separate candidate when the logs cover relevant actions. Judge each increment by closed-loop success and regret against the supervised, passive and reset policies, with identical action proposals and budgets. Preference tuning for clip naturalness or style becomes meaningful once held listeners or recognizers recover the intended content. [Learner-visited states in imitation learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html) · [Implicit Q-Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06169)

The first compute allocation I would make is the fresh-data continuation for the two existing sizes. The first behavior change I would train is the supervised action scorer on consecutive decisions. Keep those as separate experiments so a gain can be attributed to the data, the representation objective, or the policy update.
