# Frozen-state decision post-training

The small wide-state pilot produced low closed-loop occupancy for both architectures, with dense GPT-2 ahead in both seeds. This follow-up was proposed after that observation. Its new mission cohort remains separate from G9 scoring.

Use the terminal 512-coordinate GELU field from both 116M-class architectures and both training seeds. Freeze all backbone and sensory decoders. Train the same small nonlinear decision head on privileged expert action labels at training time. Compare (1) new random-action trajectories and (2) observations collected under the frozen parent policy, with the same number of environment transitions, goal changes, optimizer steps and label balancing. Include the original parent, an idle policy and the sensor-based physical controller. This isolates the sampling policy used to gather labels within this experiment. The two policies necessarily visit different observations.

Collect 128 training missions of 128 steps per arm, using disjoint initial states from the terminal test. Vary the requested station every 32 training ticks. Evaluate on 128 further fresh 512-step missions with eight sequential goals and held intermittent motor stalls. Published outputs include goal occupancy, subgoal completion, false holds and probe frequency per mission. The action head reads only the learned field. Report both training seeds and paired intervals. A gain over the parent supports inexpensive decision adaptation. A gain over random-action collection supports the hypothesis that training on visited states improves sustained operation. A gap to the physical controller remains evidence of limited control.
