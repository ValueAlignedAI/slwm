# G14 locked task and study design

A command names one of the red, blue or green blocks and one of the station labels in a public, randomized letter/digit map. Yellow is a distractor. A same-speaker quartet supplies two colors crossed with two different addresses; the two addresses differ in both letter and digit. Each quartet really contains four different original six-word recordings. No location can be inferred from the address without the map. The physical score always uses the *assigned* goal, independently of what the policy hears. Evaluate a color-only or address-only swap by rescoring its separately executed sibling branch for the original goal. Use a separate silent policy execution and an explicit transcript oracle. Supply neither transcripts nor gold word boundaries to ordinary audio decoding.

Train the acoustic edge on the previous 16 training people and validation/calibration on their separate person groups. Train unaligned audio, alignment-supervised audio, and alignment-supervised audiovisual input at seeds 1401–1403, three matched 1,536-update schedules, batch 128. Choose checkpoints and the common speaking edge by development slot cross-entropy for color, letter and digit. Assess complete recognition and true versus shifted/lost lips. The previous eight evaluation speakers and the previously reserved speakers 33 and 34 supply newly grouped utterance quartets. Exclude every exact training, calibration or development sentence from the operational held pools. Report people separately.

The native controller clock is 10 Hz. A macro contains three executed offsets and the normal single-goal cap is 24 macros (7.2 seconds). A successful target must come within 25 mm of the assigned station for three consecutive native observations. Selective success also requires every non-target block to stay within 35 mm of its own start up to success. Report ordinary arrival, final distance, unintended motion and no-op separately. Use a 160×90 RGB view with the original calibrated projection. Fit image-to-world locations on training starts alone. Independent simulator body and joint state measure outcomes. The supplied map locates four stations at separated in-bounds positions initially clear of the four blocks. Fix the layout and physics within all branches of a paired start.

Develop on at least 24 independently seeded starts. Target 128 nominal plus 128 shifted held starts across speakers 25–32; target 32 nominal plus 32 shifted starts across speakers 33–34. Use whole shared scene as the bootstrap unit, balanced within voice and condition. For each matched learned family run three seeds. Compare no-op, direct-to-object, camera-based geometric feedback, raw-signal learning and parameter-matched dense versus spatial fields. Publish an oracle-cue and oracle-perception diagnostic. The shift changes friction/mass and the mapping between color and physical shape. Retain the paired goal and exact snapshot checks. Define success for acoustically substituted or absent goals using the physical goal originally assigned.

At training time save simulator snapshots along independent demonstrations. Fork plausible waypoint/command macros and measure their effects through real contact dynamics. Fit next physical state, action or ranking, uncertainty and a commit route. Compare a raw prediction with a learned correction. If development performance reveals a mismatch, record it before a second wave and use learner-visited states or a longer measured return with a matched exposure control. Freeze the final policies and postprocessing before the held executions. A spatial architecture superiority claim requires positive paired 95% lower bounds on ordinary dense at two of three seeds in both original-speaker and new-speaker cohorts. Gains over strong feedback and raw controls are a separate question. Compare continuous retained-goal missions, reset-memory and changed-cue interventions without using their outcomes for selection.

Document data, code, uncertainty and duration for any final transfer check on recorded physical telemetry. Only the simulator supplies controlled alternative actions and task success; no unobserved physical robot outcome is claimed.
